 1. WHY DEVOPS? – DIGITAL DISRUPTION & THE NEED FOR SPEED
================================================================================

⭐ EXAM POINT: 52% of Fortune 500 companies since 2000 have disappeared due to
   disruption. It’s not “if” you get disrupted, but “when”.

DEFINITION:
+----------------------------------------------------+
| DISRUPTION = A new business model that uses existing|
| technology in an innovative way, causing incumbents |
| to lose market share rapidly.                      |
+----------------------------------------------------+

🧠 MEMORY TRICK: “Uber’s GPS, Payments, Phones” – all old tech, new model.
   Technology is the ENABLER, not the driver of innovation.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES:
  • Uber – combined GPS (old), electronic payments (older), smartphones
    (common). New model: summon a driver from an app. Disrupted taxis.
  • Netflix – started mailing DVDs (no late fees!), then streaming.
    Blockbuster thought they were in video rental; Netflix knew they were
    in entertainment. Blockbuster → bankrupt.
  • Garmin – pivoted from GPS navigation (disrupted by phones) to fitness
    trackers. Understood they were in the *tracking* business.
  • Banks – slow to update apps; competitor launches mobile check deposit
    → loses customers waiting months for their own app update.
  • Travel agents, point-and-shoot cameras, etc. – all disrupted.

⚡ INTERVIEW POINT: “Can you name a company that successfully pivoted after
   disruption?” → Garmin (mapping → tracking). “What is the real lesson from
   Blockbuster vs Netflix?” → Understand what business you are truly in.

ASCII FLOWCHART: DISRUPTION PATH
   +-------------+
   | Old Model   |   (slow, manual, high friction)
   +------+------+
          |
   Disruption by new business model + existing tech
          |
   +------v------+
   | New Model   |   (fast, digital, low friction) --> Wins market
   +-------------+

 2. TRADITIONAL WATERFALL & ITS PROBLEMS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DEFINITION:
+----------------------------------------------------+
| WATERFALL = Sequential SDLC: Requirements → Design |
| → Implementation → Verification → Maintenance.     |
| Each phase must finish before the next begins.     |
+----------------------------------------------------+

ASCII WATERFALL DIAGRAM:
   Requirements
       |
       v
   Design
       |
       v
   Implementation (coding)
       |
       v
   Verification (testing)
       |
       v
   Maintenance (deploy + run)

Problems of Waterfall:
  ⭐ No provision for change – going back is like swimming UP a waterfall.
  ⭐ No intermediate delivery – nothing works until the end.
  ⭐ Late discovery of issues → extremely costly to fix.
  ⭐ Silos: architects, developers, testers, ops all work separately,
    no mutual awareness of impact.
  ⭐ Ops gets the code last, knows the least, expected to run it.
  ⭐ Long lead times (months/years between releases).
  ⭐ Risk: “We don’t know if it works until we try to integrate at the end.”

🧠 MEMORY: “A DIME” for Waterfall problems:
   A – Absence of change flexibility
   D – Delayed integration
   I – Isolated silos
   M – Mistakes costly late
   E – End-only delivery (no mid-point value)

EXAM QUESTION:
  Q: Why is Waterfall called Waterfall?
  A: Because once a phase finishes, artifacts flow down; going back is as
     hard as swimming up a waterfall.

 3. AGILE & EXTREME PROGRAMMING – THE PREDECESSORS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1996 – Kent Beck introduces Extreme Programming (XP)
  • Iterative, feedback loops: Release (months) → Iteration (weeks) →
    Acceptance (days) → Stand-up (daily) → Pair negotiation (hourly) →
    Unit test (minutes) → Code (seconds).
  • Emphasised pair programming, test-driven development, continuous feedback.

2001 – Agile Manifesto:
   “Individuals and interactions OVER processes and tools”
   “Working software OVER comprehensive documentation”
   “Customer collaboration OVER contract negotiation”
   “Responding to change OVER following a plan”
  (While the right-side items have value, we value the left-side more.)

Key Agile Concepts:
  • Sprints (1-4 weeks)
  • Adaptive planning (plan for next 2 weeks, repeat)
  • Evolutionary development, early delivery, continuous improvement
  • Self-organising cross-functional teams

BUT: Agile alone wasn’t enough for Ops.
   ⭐ Story: Dev team Agile, built code in Feb; Ops took ticket for 3 VMs,
     said “2 weeks to find someone with 20 minutes”. Project deployed in
     September. Developers waited months → frustration.

Two-Speed IT & Shadow IT:
   Developers go around slow internal IT → spin up resources in cloud
   instantly → “Shadow IT” (unknown cloud accounts). This is how DevOps
   necessity emerged.

🧠 TRICK: “Agile made Devs fast, Ops stayed slow – DevOps bridges the gap.”

 4. WHAT EXACTLY IS DEVOPS? – DEFINITION, PRINCIPLES, MISCONCEPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OFFICIAL DEFINITION (by Patrick Debois, 2009):
+----------------------------------------------------+
| DevOps is an extension of Agile development        |
| environments that aims to enhance the process of   |
| software delivery as a whole.                      |
+----------------------------------------------------+

JOHN ROFRANO’S DEFINITION:
“DevOps is the practice of development and operations engineers working
 together during the entire development lifecycle, following Lean and
 Agile principles to deliver software in a rapid and continuous manner.”

⭐ CORE PRINCIPLES:
   1. Culture of collaboration (openness, trust, transparency)
   2. Automation of build, test, deployment (CI/CD pipelines)
   3. Lean principles (small batches, reduce waste)
   4. Measurement (data-driven decisions)
   5. Sharing (code, knowledge, responsibility)

WHAT DEVOPS IS **NOT** (Critical for Exam & Interview):
   ⚡ NOT just Dev and Ops working together while remaining in silos.
   ⚡ NOT a separate team (like “DevOps team”). You don’t “do” Agile with an
      Agile team; the whole company becomes Agile. Similarly, you become DevOps.
   ⚡ NOT a tool (Jenkins, Docker, Kubernetes are just enablers).
   ⚡ NOT a one-size-fits-all strategy (depends on product/service type).
   ⚡ NOT just automation – automating Ops alone doesn’t change culture.

🧠 MEMORY: “DevOps is NOT T.A.S.T.E.”:
   T – Tool (only)
   A – A separate team
   S – Silo cooperation without change
   T – Trick (quick fix)
   E – Exactly one recipe

 5. THE DEVOPS CULTURE – TRUST, TRANSPARENCY, COMMUNICATION, …
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

⭐ The #1 success factor in DevOps is CULTURE (not tools, not process).
   Atlassian: “Culture is the number-one success factor... shared responsibility,
   transparency, and faster feedback is the foundation of high-performing teams.”

Key Cultural Elements (Chad Dickerson, Etsy CTO):
   Trust, Transparency, Communication, Discipline, Coordination
   (all teams working for a common goal, not in silos).

Changing culture requires changing:
   1. How people THINK  → social coding, sharing, unlearning old habits
   2. How people WORK    → small batches, TDD, BDD, CI/CD
   3. How people ORGANISE→ cross-functional teams, organisational impact on
                           architecture (Conway’s Law implicit)
   4. How people MEASURE → “You get what you measure.” Use actionable metrics,
                           not vanity metrics. Measure recovery, not failure.

🧠 MEMORY CARD: “CULTURE CHANGE = T.W.O.M.” (Think, Work, Organise, Measure).

Gartner prediction: Through 2022, 75% of DevOps initiatives fail to meet
expectations due to issues around organisational learning and change – NOT
technology/skills.

 6. APPLICATION EVOLUTION – FROM MONOLITH TO MICROSERVICES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ASCII EVOLUTION DIAGRAM:
   Waterfall / Monolith on Physical Servers
       |
       v
   Agile / SOA (Service-Oriented Architecture) on Virtual Machines
       |
       v
   DevOps / Microservices on Immutable Containers (Cloud)

Key Characteristics:
   • Microservices – loosely coupled, communicate via REST APIs, can be
     deployed independently. E.g., Spotify’s recommendation engine is a
     microservice; updating it doesn’t crash the whole app.
   • Containers – ephemeral (“lasting short time”), fast startup, developer-
     centric. If a container breaks, kill it and start a new one (immutable).
   • Infrastructure as Code (IaC) – automate provisioning.
   • Automated Pipelines (CI/CD) – automate build, test, deploy.
   • Immutable Infrastructure – never modify a running server/container;
     replace with a new version.

🧠 “M.C.I.A.” – Microservices, Containers, IaC, Automation.
   “To deploy 10x/day, you need microservices, and then you need automation
    to manage them.”

 7. THREE DIMENSIONS & THREE PILLARS OF DEVOPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three Dimensions of DevOps (order of importance):
   1. CULTURE    ← most critical, hardest to change
   2. METHOD     (processes, practices like Scrum/Kanban, CI/CD)
   3. TOOLS      (Jenkins, Git, Docker... – only support the culture)

ASCII PYRAMID:
       /\
      /  \
     / TOOLS \
    /----------\
   /   METHOD   \
  /--------------\
 /    CULTURE     \
/__________________\

Three Pillars of Agility:
   Pillar 1: DevOps         – cultural change, automation, IaC, immutable
                               infrastructure
   Pillar 2: Microservices  – loosely coupled design, REST APIs, fail fast
   Pillar 3: Containers     – portable runtimes, ephemeral, fast startup

Combined they create “The Perfect Storm” for rapid, safe delivery.

⚡ INTERVIEW QUESTIONS & ANSWERS:
   Q1: What is DevOps?
   A1: It’s a cultural and professional movement that stresses communication,
       collaboration, and integration between software developers and IT
       operations. It’s not a tool or a team; it’s a mindset.

   Q2: Can you buy DevOps?
   A2: No. You can buy tools that support DevOps practices, but without
       cultural change (trust, transparency, shared goals), the initiative
       will fail. Gartner: 75% fail due to organisational learning issues.

   Q3: Give an example of a company that does DevOps well.
   A3: Etsy – deployed 517 times in January 2011, every ~25 minutes,
       based on trust, transparency, and discipline across teams. Or
       Target: deploy time reduced from 3 months to minutes.

   Q4: What is “Shift Left”?
   (Not explicit in transcript, but related) – Moving testing, security,
   and operations concerns earlier in the development lifecycle.

   Q5: What’s the difference between Continuous Delivery and Continuous
       Deployment?...
   (Briefly: Delivery ensures code is always in a deployable state;
    Deployment automatically releases every change to production.)
